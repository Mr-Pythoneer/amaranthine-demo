A few minutes a day with her family’s photos, for a grandparent with memory loss.

Two phones, one family. Rose (left) is the grandmother. Anna (right) is her daughter, who set the app up. Both are live: tap them like real phones.

Or try it yourself

  1. Rose’s daily visit Four of her own family photos. Tap any name: whatever she picks, she’s shown who it is, never told she’s wrong.
  2. Ask about her family Tap “Who are my grandchildren?”. The answer is read aloud, and it comes only from what Anna entered.
  3. The family’s side Anna sees everything the app knows about her mother, and can fix or remove any of it.

How it works

The family sets her up once: 24 short questions and some photos, about ten minutes.

The app turns that into a three-minute daily visit of her own photos. It never keeps score, and it quietly brings back the faces she’s starting to forget.

She can ask about her family any time, out loud. If the app doesn’t know, it says “Let’s ask your family”, and the question shows up on Anna’s phone.

A prototype: the AI and backend here are simple stand-ins running in your browser. The family is made up, and the photos are licensed stock and public-domain images.
